How to choose

Pick one. It becomes the whole site.

Scroll all four homepages top to bottom first — this is a visual call, and the pages above are the evidence. One board wins, and its palette, type, layout and motion become every page of the site: homepage, tour pages, booking flow, footer. Nothing gets mixed. (Good ideas from losing boards — say B’s annotated Saturday — can be rebuilt later inside the winner’s own style, so the site still reads as one hand. That’s a normal build step, not a styling decision you need to make now.)

Board AModern Heritage

Warm serif and cream in the Cheeky Kiwi tradition, executed with the discipline and motion they lack. The most complete, least risky answer — familiar to Sam on sight, visibly a class above his employer.

Board BField Notes

The guide’s notebook: polaroids, stamps, handwritten margins. The warmest and most personal of the four — and the annotated Saturday is the single most persuasive section in the bake-off. Needs discipline to avoid twee at full-site scale.

Board CBig Sky

Cinematic and image-led: full-bleed volcano, thin serif at huge scale, glass cards on dark. The strongest first impression; the coldest read. Lives or dies on having a superb photo for every hero on every page.

Board DSharp Kiwi

Price-first, grid-lined, loud. The commercial argument lands hardest here and the comparison table is instantly convincing — but warmth has to come entirely from copy and photography, and the style could belong to any modern startup.

My vote

A · Modern Heritage, sitewide

It answers the brief on every dimension at once — warm like the company, disciplined like the big operators aren’t, and modern in exactly the way Cheeky Kiwi is dated. But this is a visual call and the four pages above are the ballot: if C stops you cold and you want the whole site that cinematic, say “C” and that becomes the system. One word is enough — A, B, C or D — or mark up what you like and hate with comments directly on this page.

Copy facts on the boards (the “fourteen seasons”, prices, times) are placeholders for Sam to correct — they’re consistent across boards so only the design varies.

The dissection verdict

Cheeky Kiwi earns its 7 — and leaves the top three points on the table

Walked every key page in the browser: homepage, tour listing, the Hobbiton tour page, the crew page. The score they earn comes from brand voice carried visually, and the score they drop comes from template execution.

What earns it

  • A signature type move: a handwritten word inside a chunky warm serif headline — “New Zealand's Best Day Tours”, “Tours you'll love!”, “starts here!”
  • An owned palette: deep petrol, cream, olive, and a gold BOOK NOW you can't miss
  • A woven, tukutuku-like section divider — quiet NZ craft instead of a hard rule
  • Real crew candids: branded caps, a guide dwarfed by the giant Hobbiton hedge door. “Personal” is demonstrated, not claimed
  • Commerce lives on the page: prices on every card, a sticky date-picker with per-day prices and availability dots
  • Tour pages answer every pre-purchase question in one scroll

What caps it

  • Template bones: nearly every section is a centred stack at one width — zero compositional risk
  • No motion design at all; the only movement is lazy-load pop-in, sections arriving as white voids
  • Badge clutter: up to five TripAdvisor badges stamped over a single card photo
  • Script type goes illegible at small sizes; hero text sits on busy photos with thin scrims
  • Postcard stock and phone candids mixed with no art direction
  • Clip-art icons and a dotted world map inside an otherwise distinctive brand
Found during the audit — and it changes the diagnosis

The v1 site never loaded its fonts. The CSS declares Fraunces and Inter at /fonts/…, but the font files were only ever committed to the reference branch — the live build serves system fallbacks on every page. The site you judged “flat, plain, kind of wrong” was rendering in Georgia the whole time. This page embeds the real faces (the boards above are set in them), and v2 fixes it behind a test that fails if a font ever 404s again.

The shared vocabulary

Ten dimensions of a marketing site

The axes a designer is actually moving on. You never need the jargon — “number 4, more of that” or “A, but with C's hero energy” works fine. Each entry: what the lever does, what Cheeky Kiwi does with it, the plan for Sam's NZ.

1

Concept

The one idea every visual choice serves. Sites feel “AI-generated” or “template” precisely when there is no idea — just competent parts.

At Cheeky KiwiThe cheeky mate who knows the way — the name, the script asides, “FOLLOW THE KIWI” all pull one direction.
For Sam's NZWorking concept: your day, in good hands — two guides you meet before you book, a day built around you. Each board above dresses that idea differently.
2

Typographic voice

Which faces, how paired, at what scale — and the one signature move that makes headlines recognisably yours. Type is the fastest way a page signals craft or cheapness.

At Cheeky KiwiTheir best trick: warm chunky serif with a script word inside it. Wobbles when the script shrinks to illegibility.
For Sam's NZA serif with real character (boards A–C are set in Fraunces), one signature accent device of our own, and a strict scale so nothing wobbles.
3

Palette & temperature

Grounds (the colors pages sit on), accents (the colors that act), and whether the whole reads warm or cold. AI-slop defaults to cold gradients on white or black.

At Cheeky KiwiPetrol navy, cream, olive, gold — warm, owned, consistently applied. Genuinely good.
For Sam's NZStay warm, stay NZ. The boards stake out the range: cream-first (A), paper (B), basalt-dark (C), white-and-green (D).
4

Composition

The layout grammar: symmetric vs asymmetric, whether elements overlap and break the grid, how section widths vary. “Centred stack after centred stack” is the single biggest template tell.

At Cheeky KiwiTheir weakest axis. Almost everything is a centred stack at one container width.
For Sam's NZAsymmetry with discipline: offset heroes, overlapping photo groups, one width change per page as a moment of drama. Compare how A and B handle the same content.
5

Depth & materiality

Layering, shadow, texture, surface changes — what makes a page feel like a made object instead of a printout. Flat everything reads cheap; fake 3D reads worse.

At Cheeky KiwiThe woven divider is real materiality; the rest is flat-ish cards and one drop shadow.
For Sam's NZA surface system: dark “dream” surfaces for aspiration, light “work” surfaces for decisions, one tactile motif recurring. A's weave, B's tape and paper, C's glass show three flavours.
6

Motion grammar

Entrance choreography (how things arrive), scroll-linked motion, micro-interactions (hover, press), and restraint (honouring reduced-motion). In 2026 this is the sharpest line between modern and dated.

At Cheeky KiwiNone. Zero intentional motion. The only movement is lazy-load flicker — sections arriving as white voids. Their clearest gap.
For Sam's NZChoreographed, quiet, everywhere. Compare the boards: A's staggered arrivals, B's settling polaroids, C's cinematic drift, D's fast snap. The ↺ button in the bar replays them.
7

Imagery art direction

Not which photos — which kind of photos, held to one grade and tone. Postcard-polished sells the place; candid-imperfect sells the people. Mixing them without intent sells neither.

At Cheeky KiwiThe candid crew shots are their most persuasive asset; the postcard stock beside them is fine; the un-directed mix is the miss.
For Sam's NZTwo deliberate registers: postcard for landscapes (Taupō, the Coromandel), candid for people — never swapped. The guide slots stay empty until the licensed stand-ins are sourced; that's deliberate.
8

Proof & commerce

Where trust and price live on the page, and how loudly. Social proof, guarantees, price transparency, booking friction — conversion design, not decoration.

At Cheeky KiwiNative booking calendar with per-day prices: excellent. Badge walls five deep on every card: proof turned into noise.
For Sam's NZNo badges exist yet, so trust comes from faces, story, launch pricing, risk-reversal. Compare how loudly each board says “$219 instead of $259” — that loudness is a real decision you get to make.
9

Pacing & whitespace

The rhythm of dense and empty: how much air sections get, how long text lines run, whether the page has second-read moments — small details you only notice on the way back up.

At Cheeky KiwiDecent air, but uniform — every section breathes at the same rate, so nothing feels like an event.
For Sam's NZVary the rhythm: generous around the few things that matter (price, faces, the day itself), tight where people scan. B's stamps and margin notes are second-read moments.
10

Voice in the copy

Words as design material: kickers, headline length, button labels, microcopy. A site can look designed and still read like a brochure wrote it.

At Cheeky Kiwi“Famously passionate tour guides”, “all-around legends”, “Follow the Kiwi” — the voice is real and consistent down to the buttons.
For Sam's NZFirst person and specific. The boards share one copy voice on purpose — “Coffee stop non-negotiable”, “while we earn our reviews” — so you judge design, not words. Tell me if the voice itself needs a different temperature.
