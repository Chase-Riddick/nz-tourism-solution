# What Sam has to decide

Everything on this site that a researcher can only approximate, and that the
person actually running the business has to own.

Each item states **what the site currently assumes**, so there is something
concrete to disagree with. Disagreement is the point — a blank field gives you
nothing to react to, a wrong number gives you plenty.

Ordered by how early the decision has to be made, not by importance.

---

## 1. Before anything else — is this even the business?

| # | Decision | What the site assumes |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | **The name.** | **Slow North.** Chosen because it is a positioning statement, not decoration: a small operator cannot beat a coach on breadth or price, so it sells pace. It is English, which sidesteps 1.2. No NZ tour operator is currently using it — but **a web search is not a trademark search**, and IPONZ clearance is yours. Note "slow" may read as a negative before it reads as the category. |
| 1.2 | **Whether you want a te reo Māori name.** | The site deliberately does **not** use one. NZ guidance is explicit that te reo naming without cultural foundations is appropriation, and IPONZ runs a Māori Advisory Committee that can decline a mark on a noa/tapu basis. If you want one, the path is engagement first, registration second — not a naming exercise. Te reo *place names* are used throughout, correctly macronised; that is different. |
| 1.3 | **Base of operations.** | **Rotorua**, working out of Auckland too. This drives which tours are day-trippable and which need accommodation. |
| 1.4 | **Team size.** | **Two to four people**, one vehicle, one guide per departure. Every operational limit on the site follows from this. |
| 1.5 | **Whether the East Cape tour is real.** | It is on the site as a 5-day, $3,150 differentiator. It is also the hardest to run: ~1,100km, remote, thin accommodation. It may be the best thing here or the first thing to cut. |

## 2. Commercial

| # | Decision | What the site assumes |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | **Every price.** | Day tours $265–$545; multi-day ~$565–630 per person per day. Triangulated in `docs/research/pricing-and-products.md` against Bush and Beach ($510 for a 12-hour Hobbiton+Waitomo day), Wild Kiwi (~$283/day at 16 seats), a private driver-guide (~$950/day for two), and an independent $269/day mid-range benchmark. **These are the numbers most likely to be wrong.** |
| 2.2 | **Maximum group size.** | **8.** The whole pricing argument rests on it — roughly double a 16-seat coach, roughly a third of a private guide. Change this and every price changes. |
| 2.3 | **Peak supplement.** | **+15%** on multi-day, December–February. Research says underlying accommodation and vehicle costs rise 20–50%, so this deliberately absorbs part of it. |
| 2.4 | **Child pricing.** | ~55% of adult on day tours; children not taken on alpine or wine tours. |
| 2.5 | **Single supplement.** | $210–$540 depending on trip length. |
| 2.6 | **Deposit and balance.** | 25% deposit, balance 30 days out. |
| 2.7 | **Cancellation terms.** | >30 days full refund less $50; 15–30 days 50%; ≤14 days nothing; weather cancellation by us = full refund or transfer, guest's choice. Industry-shaped, not yours. |
| 2.8 | **Booking platform.** | **None chosen.** The site models it as one constant. Rezdy / Bókun / Checkfront / FareHarbor are the realistic options; the choice is commercial (commission, OTA distribution, wholesaler connectivity) and it decides your spam handling too. |
| 2.9 | **Minimum numbers.** | Two bookings to run a multi-day departure, 21 days' notice if not met. |

## 3. Legal and regulatory — the expensive ones

| # | Decision | What the site assumes |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | **Entity and NZBN.** | Shown as a placeholder. No number is invented — a plausible NZBN is a forged government identifier. |
| 3.2 | **GST registration.** | Site states all prices are GST inclusive. |
| 3.3 | **DOC concession — and of which class.** | **The single highest-consequence unknown.** A concession is required to operate commercially on public conservation land. Four tours touch it (`west-coast-beaches`, `tongariro-crossing`, `central-plateau`, `northland-three`, `east-cape-five`). Passenger transport operators need one; those in dangerous terrain or providing an adventure activity may also need an Adventure Activity Audit. **These itineraries cannot be sold until this is resolved.** |
| 3.4 | **Adventure-activity classification.** | Guided walking, caving and kayaking sit near the boundary of the Health and Safety at Work (Adventure Activities) Regulations 2016. Where a tour falls decides whether the full WorkSafe audit-and-registration regime applies. Get each activity classified before publishing it. |
| 3.5 | **Qualmark.** | Worth pursuing early for a non-obvious reason: accreditation is accepted as **evidence of a safety management plan in a DOC concession application**. One process satisfies part of another. |
| 3.6 | **Public liability insurance.** | Required by most venues and all wholesalers before they will contract. |
| 3.7 | **P endorsement.** | Required to carry paying passengers. Non-negotiable. |
| 3.8 | **Privacy policy review.** | Drafted against the NZ Privacy Act 2020 with GDPR rights honoured equivalently. Not written by a lawyer. |

## 4. Operational

| # | Decision | What the site assumes |
|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | **Guide rest days.** | NZ law requires them after a given number of working hours, and this is **the binding constraint on how many consecutive multi-day departures you can run**. The site shows set departures rather than daily availability because of it. |
| 4.2 | **The vehicle.** | One, seating 8 guests plus guide. Not specified further. |
| 4.3 | **Venue relationships.** | Hobbiton, Waitomo, Te Puia and the Tongariro shuttle operators are all ticketed third parties. Trade rates and booking arrangements are yours to negotiate — and they change the day-tour margins materially. |
| 4.4 | **Accommodation blocks.** | Multi-day tours assume twin-share at a mid-range standard. Not booked, not costed against real rates. |
| 4.5 | **Pickup points.** | Site says city-centre accommodation pickup on day tours. That is generous and it costs time. |
| 4.6 | **Whether you run winter at all.** | The site commits to year-round with a rotating product — Northland and geothermal in winter, alpine October–May. That is a real commitment to staffing a low season. |

## 5. Cultural

| # | Decision | What the site assumes |
|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | **Any iwi relationship.** | The site asserts **none**, deliberately. Cultural experiences are described as operated by their owners rather than as something Slow North provides. If you want to offer anything more, that is a relationship to build, not a product to list. |
| 5.2 | **Cape Rēinga framing.** | Described as significant in Māori understanding as the place where spirits depart. Check the wording with someone who should be checking it. |
| 5.3 | **Macrons.** | Used throughout and asserted by tests. Verify the specific names against how local iwi write them. |

## 6. Brand and languages

| # | Decision | What the site assumes |
|---|---|---|
| 6.1 | **Which languages you can actually service.** | **English only**, and the FAQ says so bluntly rather than implying more. German is the researched next locale — 74% of German arrivals come for a holiday against ~48% market-wide, highest spend per trip, longest stay, and their most-enjoyed activity is walks and hikes. It ships only when you can answer German email. |
| 6.2 | **Chinese.** | Deferred as a *channel* problem, not a translation one: the market is heavily intermediated, and serving it properly means WeChat, Xiaohongshu and a domestic booking channel. If you land a wholesaler relationship, revisit. |
| 6.3 | **Every social handle.** | Placeholders. None of these accounts exist. **Register them before launch — a dead social link is worse than none.** |
| 6.4 | **Contact details.** | All placeholders, including the email and phone. |

## 7. Photography — what the site cannot show

| # | Decision | What the site assumes |
|---|---|---|
| 7.1 | **There is not one photograph of a person on this site.** | Deliberate. No model releases are available for Commons imagery, so every "your guide" and "our guests" slot is absent rather than faked. **One afternoon with a real camera and signed releases fixes this permanently**, and it is the single highest-value photographic addition available. |
| 7.2 | **The vehicle.** | Same. A photograph of the actual van, badged, is worth more for trust than any landscape currently on the site. |
| 7.3 | **Waitomo.** | Only 2 usable frames from 8 fetched — Commons has almost no good glowworm-cave photography and operators restrict it. A venue relationship gives access to their media library. That is the actual fix. |
| 7.4 | **Whether to keep Commons imagery at all.** | 38 of 49 published images require attribution and 32 are share-alike. Your own photographs remove that obligation entirely. |

| A.1 | **Launch pricing holds at these entry rates.** | Every tour priced 15–20% under Cheeky Kiwi's published comparable (`docs/research/pricing-v2-cheekykiwi.md`).
