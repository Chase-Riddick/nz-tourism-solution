# Seasons, places, and what can honestly be sold when

Fetched 2026-08-18. New Zealand is in the Southern Hemisphere: **December–February is
summer**. Getting this backwards, or selling an alpine crossing in July, is the fastest
way to lose a New Zealand reader.

## The seasonal frame

| Season | Months | Temps | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Summer** | Dec–Feb | 20–24°C | Peak. Best hiking weather, busiest, most expensive (+20–50% on accom/vehicle). Book early. |
| **Autumn** | Mar–May | 17–22°C | Mild, thinning crowds. **March is a shoulder sweet spot.** |
| **Winter** | Jun–Aug | 10–15°C | Wettest. Northland is "the winterless north" and stays workable. Alpine terrain closes. |
| **Spring** | Sep–Nov | 16–20°C | Rising temps, young wildlife. **November is arguably the best month** — consistently sunny, few travellers. |

The North Island has **no ski-driven winter peak** (Ruapehu aside). So the business is
genuinely year-round, but the *product mix* has to rotate rather than the calendar
simply repeating. That rotation is the honest version of "tours across the year", and
the site is built around it.

## The hard seasonal constraint: Tongariro Alpine Crossing

Widely called New Zealand's best single-day hike, and the anchor of any serious
central-North-Island itinerary. It is also **weather-gated and shuttle-dependent**:

- **Shuttle season is October to May.** Departures 6am–10am.
- **Once snow covers the track — usually June or July — shuttle operators stop
  running** until conditions are safe.
- **Bad-weather closure is a concession matter, not just advice.** DOC issues a strong
  recommendation not to hike, and **approved shuttle operators must stop operating
  under their DOC concession.** An operator cannot decide to run it anyway.
- **Winter crossing requires alpine skills** — snow, ice, avalanche terrain, sub-zero
  temperatures — and a guide is strongly recommended.
- **Track bookings are required** for shuttle and guided services.

### What this forces on the site

1. The Crossing appears as an **October–May** product. Never year-round.
2. Its page carries a **plain weather-cancellation policy**, because cancellation is
   normal here, not exceptional — and saying so is a credibility marker to anyone who
   knows the mountain.
3. Winter itineraries route to **coast, geothermal and forest** instead — which is
   exactly what a real operator does.
4. The 2026 booking detail found during research (transport/camping bookings closed for
   winter, reopening 7 November 2026 for summer 26/27) confirms the rhythm: the alpine
   season is **sold in advance and closed in winter**.

## Places, and why each is on the itinerary list

Chosen for what a 2–4 person operator can genuinely reach on a 1–5 day loop from
Auckland or Rotorua — not a national highlights list.

**Day-trip radius (ex-Auckland / ex-Rotorua)**
- **Hobbiton (Matamata)** — the single strongest commercial draw; every comparable
  operator runs it. Ticketed, and a venue relationship is required.
- **Waitomo** — glowworm caves, boat through the grotto. Ticketed.
- **Rotorua** — geothermal (Te Puia, Wai-O-Tapu), Māori cultural evening, redwoods.
  The geothermal product is genuinely all-weather, which makes it the winter backbone.
- **Coromandel** — Cathedral Cove, Hot Water Beach. Kayaking. Summer-weighted.
- **Waiheke** — vineyards, ferry-accessible from Auckland. No vehicle day needed.
- **Waitakere Ranges / west coast beaches** — Piha, black sand. Close to Auckland.
  *Kauri dieback restrictions apply and track closures are real — verify before selling.*

**Multi-day radius**
- **Tongariro National Park** — the Crossing (Oct–May), Whakapapa, Taranaki views.
- **Taupō** — Huka Falls, the lake, geothermal. Natural overnight hinge between
  Rotorua and Tongariro.
- **Bay of Islands / Northland** — Waitangi, Hokianga, Cape Rēinga. **The winter
  product**, given "the winterless north".
- **East Cape** — Te Araroa, Tolaga Bay, first light in the world at Gisborne. Remote,
  long driving days, genuinely uncrowded. A differentiator, but demanding on a small
  fleet.
- **Whirinaki / Te Urewera** — old-growth forest. **Conservation land — concession
  class matters.**

## Māori place names

Macrons are correct and required: **Taupō, Tongariro, Whakatāne, Ōpōtiki, Te Rēinga,
Whirinaki, Te Urewera, Rēinga.** The font stack must render them and every content file
is UTF-8. Getting a macron wrong is the written equivalent of mispronouncing the place.

Anything implying **iwi partnership** or a **cultural experience** is a relationship Sam
must actually hold, not a product a website can assert. Those are `SAM-VERIFY` items,
and the site describes cultural experiences as **operated by their owners** rather than
as something this operator provides.
