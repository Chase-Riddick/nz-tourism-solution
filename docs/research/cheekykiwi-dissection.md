# Cheeky Kiwi Travel — full-site dissection

Source: https://www.cheekykiwitravel.com (walked in-browser, screenshots, 2026-08-19).
Chase's rating: 7–8/10. Appointed reference for the v2 redesign; Sam guides here.
Note: cheekykiwi.com (bare) is an unrelated parked domain.

## What earns the 7–8 — patterns worth learning from

1. **A voice carried visually.** "Guided. Personal. Authentic." / "FOLLOW THE
   KIWI" / "Your New Zealand adventure starts *here!*" — the cheek is in the
   type, not just the words. Signature device: a handwritten-script word set
   inside a chunky warm serif headline ("Best", "love!", "Magic", "here!"),
   often with a hand-drawn underline swash.
2. **Distinct, warm palette.** Deep petrol navy grounds, cream paper, olive
   green accents, mustard-gold CTAs. Dark and light sections alternate in a
   deliberate rhythm; the gold pill "BOOK NOW" is unmissable in every context.
3. **NZ-craft texture.** A herringbone/tukutuku-like woven divider between
   sections — a quiet cultural motif doing the work of a hard rule.
4. **Real people, imperfect photos.** Crew page: branded caps, phone-quality
   candids (guide dwarfed by the giant Hobbiton hedge door; selfie with guests
   on black sand). Reads as proof, not decoration. The "personal" claim is
   *demonstrated*.
5. **Commerce is native to the page.** Prices on every card ("From NZD$315");
   tour pages carry a sticky "Select a date" calendar with per-day prices and
   green/amber availability dots (Rezdy-backed). The page is the booking engine.
6. **Tour page answers everything.** Breadcrumb → photo hero with price →
   gallery → story copy → arrow-bullet highlights → itinerary → YouTube embed →
   why-us → dated TripAdvisor review cards that name the guide (Grant, Jeoff).
7. **Proof accumulation.** Badge walls (8 TripAdvisor years + Qualmark Gold +
   Tiaki + Tourism Export Council), ratings with review counts on every card.

## What keeps it from 9–10 — the gap v2 exploits

1. **Template bones.** Visibly a page-builder build: nearly every section is a
   centred stack at one container width, same heading-then-content cadence. No
   compositional risk anywhere.
2. **Zero motion design.** No entrance choreography, no scroll-linked motion,
   no hover life beyond browser defaults. The only movement is lazy-load
   pop-in — sections arrive as white voids and fill a beat later (map
   illustration, tour cards, crew grid all visibly assemble). This is the
   single clearest 2018-vs-2026 tell.
3. **Badge clutter.** Up to five overlapping TripAdvisor badges stamped over
   the hero photo of every tour card. Trust rendered as noise; hierarchy
   sacrificed to accumulation.
4. **Type discipline wobbles.** Script at small sizes is near-illegible
   ("Stories worth sharing"); abrupt scale jumps; listing-page hero sets a
   huge headline over a busy photo with too little scrim.
5. **Stock-vs-candid whiplash.** Polished postcard Hobbiton/Milford stock next
   to phone snapshots with no unifying grade or art direction.
6. **Off-the-shelf ornament.** Clip-art line icons in circles, dotted world
   map, default chat bubble — generic texture inside a distinctive brand.
7. **Awkward horizontal carousels** with a thin progress line; tall cards hide
   inventory and scroll poorly.

## Strategic note for Sam's NZ

Cheeky Kiwi's design leans hard on accumulated proof (badges, counts, awards).
Sam's NZ has none of that at launch — so the v2 design must produce trust from
**people, story, craft, and price clarity** instead. Their weakest layer
(motion, composition, art direction) is exactly where a new site can visibly
outclass them; their strongest layer (native commerce, complete tour pages,
personality in type) is what we match.
